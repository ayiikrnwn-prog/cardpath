The details

A card that behaves like a loan — fixed payments, no surprises.


Revolving credit is where rebuilders get hurt: a balance that lingers, interest that snowballs, a score that dips with utilization. The Upgrade Card redesigns the mechanics — every balance you carry becomes a fixed-payment installment plan, reported as personal loans, not revolving debt.

For people who got into trouble precisely because credit cards are too flexible, the rigidity is the feature.

How it works

Apply once; if approved you get a line you draw against. Each draw becomes an installment with fixed monthly payments and a fixed payoff date. On-time payments report to all three bureaus. There is no annual fee; APR depends on your credit profile at approval. Because utilization on the card does not swing your score the way revolving balances do, the score path is calmer.

Who it's for — and who should look elsewhere

Good fit if you:
  • Prefer fixed payments over revolving flexibility
  • Rebuilding with steady income
  • Want credit-building without deposit
Look elsewhere if you:
  • Want traditional swipe-anywhere rewards (Quicksilver Secured)
  • No score and no income (secured no-check options first)
  • Active bankruptcy (OpenSky)

FAQ

Frequently asked questions


Does checking or applying hurt my credit score?
Checking your offer uses a soft pull. Accepting and drawing involves a hard inquiry for most applicants.
What credit score do I need?
Upgrade targets fair-and-better profiles (roughly 560+ in practice). Poor/no credit usually starts with secured or no-check products.
How long does it take to build credit with this card?
Installment history builds steadily — expect measurable impact over 6+ months of on-time payments.
What happens to my deposit?
There is no deposit — the product is unsecured with fixed installment draws.
Can I be denied?
Yes — underwriting is income- and history-based, stricter than no-check secured cards.
