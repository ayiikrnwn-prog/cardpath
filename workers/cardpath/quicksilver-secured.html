The details

Cash back while you rebuild — not either-or.


For years the rule was simple: secured cards build credit, rewards cards pay you. You picked one. Capital One broke the rule — the Quicksilver Secured pays unlimited 1.5% cash back on every purchase while your file grows underneath it.

If you are rebuilding, every dollar of spending you already do — gas, groceries, subscriptions — can work twice: once as the payment history that raises your score, once as cash back in your pocket.

How it works

You fund a $200 refundable deposit, which becomes your credit line. From the first swipe, purchases earn 1.5% cash back with no categories to track and no annual fee. Payments report to all three bureaus. Over time, Capital One reviews accounts for credit line increases and potential graduation to the unsecured Quicksilver — where the rewards structure continues.

Who it's for — and who should look elsewhere

Good fit if you:
  • Rebuilding with regular monthly spending
  • Can fund a $200 deposit
  • Want rewards while building, not after
Look elsewhere if you:
  • Cannot put $200 aside (Capital One Platinum Secured starts at $49)
  • No score at all and no bank account (OpenSky)
  • Maximum upgrade speed (Discover has auto reviews at 7 months)

FAQ

Frequently asked questions


Does checking or applying hurt my credit score?
Pre-qualification uses a soft check with no score impact. A formal application involves a credit check, but as a secured card, approval centers on your deposit rather than your score.
What credit score do I need?
No published minimum — it targets fair, limited, or rebuilding credit. The $200 deposit is the real requirement.
How long does it take to build credit with this card?
Reporting starts with your first billing cycle. Expect meaningful movement after 3–6 months; Capital One periodically reviews accounts for upgrades.
What happens to my deposit?
Refundable — it becomes your credit line. On graduation to unsecured or closure in good standing, you get it back.
Can I be denied?
Yes. Denied applications typically involve insufficient income, recent delinquencies, or application volume. Pre-qualifying first avoids surprises.
