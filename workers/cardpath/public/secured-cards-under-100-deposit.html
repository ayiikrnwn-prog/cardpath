Secured cards under $100 deposit — the real list.

Most secured cards want $200. Four genuine options start below $100 — here is what each trade costs you.

The $200 minimum is the most common wall in credit rebuilding. Below $100, the options narrow fast — and one of them is not what it appears. Here is the honest list of cards that start under $100.

Spoiler: the cheapest deposit is rarely the cheapest card.

How it works

The list: (1) Capital One Platinum Secured — deposit from $49 (Capital One assigns $49/$99/$200 by approval); $0 annual fee; no rewards; 6-month line reviews. (2) Chime Credit Builder — $0 deposit (you move money as you spend); requires the Chime checking account; no credit check. (3) OpenSky — $100 minimum (right at the line); $35 fee; no credit check, bankruptcy-friendly. (4) Self Secured Visa — no cash deposit (loan-funded); requires the Credit Builder Account.

Who each option fits

Good fit if you:
  • Budgets under $100 for one-time deposit
  • No bank account (OpenSky) or no cash (Self/Chime)
  • First-ever card
Look elsewhere if you:
  • Want rewards — under-$100 options skip rewards by design
  • Want big credit lines immediately (deposit size = line size)

The honest comparison

Capital One is the best pure value under $100 ($0 fee, major bank). Chime wins for zero total cost if the checking account works for you. OpenSky is the fallback when the first two doors close. Avoid "low deposit" offers from subprime issuers with annual fees — the fee eats the deposit advantage within a year.

What to watch out for

A practical warning: "low deposit" offers from subprime issuers usually hide annual fees that erase the deposit advantage within the first year. If a card under $100 deposit carries a $75+ fee, the math is worse than saving another month to fund a $0-fee option. Also worth knowing: deposit size equals credit line, so a $49 line means a $15 grocery run is 30% utilization. Two mitigations exist — pay before the statement date (the reported balance is what matters), or grow the line via Capital One's 6-month review. Budget builders should treat the first deposit as a floor, not a ceiling.

Practical next step

Practical next step: if your budget is fixed under $100, decide which constraint binds first — the deposit itself, the bank account requirement, or the annual fee. Each of the four cards above removes exactly one constraint; matching your binding constraint to the right card matters more than any feature comparison.

Frequently asked questions

Does checking or applying hurt my credit score?
Chime and OpenSky apply with no credit check; Capital One pre-qualification is a soft pull.
What credit score do I need?
No minimums on any of the four — deposit funding and identity verification drive approval.
How long does it take to build credit with this card?
All report monthly; 3–6 months of on-time activity is the standard movement window.
What happens to my deposit?
Refundable in all cases — Chime's is your own spendable money by design.
Can I be denied?
Yes — Capital One and Self underwrite; Chime and OpenSky deny mainly on identity/funding verification.
