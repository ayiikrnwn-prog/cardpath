The details

After bankruptcy, when most banks close the door.


A bankruptcy filing closes most credit doors for years. OpenSky exists for exactly that moment: no credit check to apply, and issuers' usual bankruptcy exclusions do not apply here — applicants with active or discharged bankruptcies are considered.

That makes OpenSky the most common second-chance card in rebuild-after-setback journeys — not the cheapest, but the one most likely to say yes.

How it works

You fund a refundable deposit between $100 and $3,000 — it becomes your credit line. No credit check is performed. Payments report to all three bureaus from the start. The $35 annual fee is the trade-off for the flexible approval; there is no interest as long as you pay your statement balance. Upgrade to unsecured is possible after a history of on-time payments.

Who it's for — and who should look elsewhere

Good fit if you:
  • Active or discharged bankruptcy
  • No bank account (fund by money order or debit)
  • Denied by major issuers repeatedly
Look elsewhere if you:
  • Want $0 annual fee (Chime, if a checking account fits you)
  • Want rewards (Quicksilver Secured)
  • Have fair credit and no bankruptcy history (better options exist)

How it compares

Versus the Self Secured Visa: Self requires no cash upfront (it builds through a credit-builder loan), which suits tighter budgets; OpenSky is faster to a usable credit line and more established for bankruptcy cases. Versus Chime: Chime has no fee but requires a checking account with direct deposit.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions


Does checking or applying hurt my credit score?
No — OpenSky performs no credit check at application. Approval is based on your deposit and identity verification.
What credit score do I need?
None. There is no score requirement, and bankruptcy does not disqualify you — the deposit is the collateral.
How long does it take to build credit with this card?
Reporting starts with your first cycle. With bankruptcy on file, expect a longer arc — 6–12 months of on-time reporting before meaningful movement.
What happens to my deposit?
It is refundable — returned if you close in good standing or upgrade to unsecured.
Can I be denied?
Rarely, but yes — identity verification failure, deposit funding failure, or application volume can lead to denial.
