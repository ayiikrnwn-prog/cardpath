The details

No deposit, no collateral — at a price you should see coming.


Every secured card asks the same thing: cash upfront. If you have $0 to spare, that is a wall. Credit One Platinum removes it — no deposit, unsecured, aimed at fair or rebuilding credit.

The trade is honesty about a fee: $75 in year one, then $99 annually (billed $8.25 monthly). For some rebuilders that fee is worth skipping the deposit; for others it is exactly why the secured path wins. We show both sides because trust beats a surprise statement.

How it works

There is no deposit to fund. You apply, Credit One checks your credit, and if approved you get a modest unsecured line — often starting around $300–$500. Payments report to all three major bureaus. Cash back (1% on eligible purchases) applies on some versions of this card; check your offer terms.

Who it's for — and who should look elsewhere

Good fit if you:
  • Zero dollars available for a deposit
  • Fair credit with some history
  • Value unsecured over low cost
Look elsewhere if you:
  • Can fund $49+ — the Capital One Platinum Secured is fee-free and cheaper long-term
  • No score and no bank account (OpenSky)
  • Active bankruptcy (OpenSky again)

How it compares

Versus Capital One Platinum Secured: Credit One needs no deposit but costs $75–99/year forever; Capital One needs a refundable deposit and costs $0/year. Over two years, the secured path saves $150–198 and returns the deposit. Choose Credit One only when the deposit is truly impossible.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions


Does checking or applying hurt my credit score?
Applying involves a hard credit check — there is no soft pre-qualification for this product. One application, one inquiry.
What credit score do I need?
Targeted at fair credit (roughly 580–669 range, though no official minimum is published). Poor or no credit usually needs the secured or no-credit-check routes.
How long does it take to build credit with this card?
Reporting begins with the first cycle; movement typically appears within 3–6 months of on-time payments.
What happens to my deposit?
There is no deposit — this card is unsecured. The cost is the annual fee instead.
Can I be denied?
Yes — this is a real unsecured approval with hard-pull underwriting, so denial rates are meaningfully higher than secured options.
