The details

Student? This is the rare card that says yes without a history.


Students face a paradox: no card means no history, no history means no card. Issuer student cards break the loop by underwriting on enrollment and income rather than score — and Discover's student card is the most generous of the category: real rewards, no annual fee, and a score tracker built in.

Start in college and you graduate with 4+ years of payment history — the single largest score factor — already banked.

How it works

Apply with proof of enrollment and some income (scholarships and part-time work count). If approved, you get an unsecured line with 5% cash back in rotating quarterly categories (activation required) and 1% on everything else — first-year cash back matched automatically. No annual fee, no penalty APR on the first late payment (a genuinely useful grace for first-time cardholders), and free FICO score monitoring.

Who it's for — and who should look elsewhere

Good fit if you:
  • Enrolled student with any income source
  • First credit card ever
  • Want rewards without a deposit
Look elsewhere if you:
  • Not enrolled (secured cards are the path)
  • Want a flat rate with no category tracking (Quicksilver-style)
  • Rebuilding with negatives (student underwriting is for clean thin files)

FAQ

Frequently asked questions


Does checking or applying hurt my credit score?
Pre-qualification is available with a soft check. Applying involves a hard inquiry — but for students, a single inquiry at the start of a long file is a small cost.
What credit score do I need?
There is no published minimum — underwriting weighs enrollment and income. Many approvals happen with no score at all.
How long does it take to build credit with this card?
First-cycle reporting. Students who start freshman year typically graduate with strong prime-ready files.
What happens to my deposit?
No deposit — unsecured. The card may upgrade or convert as your profile matures.
Can I be denied?
Yes — typically for insufficient income or enrollment verification failure.
