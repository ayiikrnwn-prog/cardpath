10 Ways to Build Credit Without a Credit Card — Ranked by Cost, Speed, and Bureau Coverage

A credit card is the most common credit-building tool — and far from the only one. For renters, the unbanked, or anyone burned by cards before, these paths build real reported history without ever swiping. Ranked by what they cost, how fast they move, and which bureaus they reach.

1. Rent reporting — the biggest untapped bill

BoomPay ($2/month) reports rent to all 3 bureaus and can back-report up to 24 months of history. For most credit-insecure Americans, rent is the largest bill they pay — and it never counted. Until now. Speed: first tradeline in weeks, back-history counts immediately. Cost: $24/year. This is the highest value-per-dollar in the entire no-card toolbox. CFPB research supports rent tradelines meaningfully lifting thin-file scores.

2. Credit-builder loans — installment history

Self ($25-150/month, savings returned at completion), Credit Strong ($15-28/month, savings returned, plans to $25k), Kikoff ($5/month, no money back — subscription cost). CFPB data: builder loans meaningfully raise score odds for thin files. They add the installment category (10% of FICO) that cards cannot. Pick Self or Credit Strong when the savings-return matters; Kikoff only when $5/mo is the whole budget.

3. Experian Boost / Experian Go — free and instant

Connects your bank and adds utility, phone, and streaming payments to your Experian file — average +13 FICO 8 points, instantly. Experian Go can create a file from nothing if you have qualifying bills. Honest limit: Experian only — Equifax and TransUnion never see it. Still: free, instant, zero risk. Start here while other tools spin up.

4. Grow Credit — build with bills you already pay

A virtual Mastercard that pays your existing streaming and phone subscriptions; the payments report. Free tier exists. It converts subscriptions you cannot cancel anyway into reported payment history. Weakness: small amounts, small lines — a complement, not a foundation.

5. Authorized user — the classic with fine print

Being added to a family member’s old card in good standing can add years of history to your file. Free and fast. Fine print: the issuer must report AU history (most do), their late payments become YOUR late payments, and the benefit fades on newer FICO models for AU accounts opened recently. Best case: a parent’s 10-year-old, never-late card. Worst case: their maxed-out month becomes your problem.

6. Credit-union products — the offline path

PAL loans ($200-2,000 at APR 5-18%, capped by regulation) and CD-secured loans from local credit unions — often better priced than fintech. Junto Avanzamos credit unions specialize in ITIN lending with Spanish-language service — a real option our decision tree covers for newcomers.

7-10. The rest of the toolbox

(7) Tradeline companies — we do not recommend them: paid AU spots are expensive, risky, and arguably fraudulent misrepresentation. (8) Secured cards with $0 deposit — Chime/Varo, covered in our deposit guide, need a bank relationship. (9) Reporting student-loan payments — they already count if current; nothing to add. (10) Simply waiting — time alone ages a file, but it is the slowest tool in the box.

The combination that actually works

Boost (free, instant) + rent reporting ($2/mo, all 3 bureaus) + one builder loan (installment mix) covers instant points, your biggest bill, and the missing category — under $30/month total, no card required. Add a spend-only card later when a bank relationship makes sense. This stack is what our quiz recommends for income-tight, no-card profiles.

Frequently asked questions

Do these build all 3 bureaus?
Rent reporting and builder loans: all 3. Experian Boost: Experian only. AU history: depends on the issuer.
What is the fastest no-card method?
Experian Boost — instant. VantageScore can score new files in 1-2 months; FICO still needs 6.
Can I combine multiple methods?
Yes — the best stack does: Boost + rent + builder loan covers three factors at once.
Do any require a deposit?
No — that is the point of this list. Nothing here locks cash.
Are tradeline companies legit?
We do not recommend them: expensive, against issuer terms, and misrepresentation risk. Use the free or low-cost paths instead.
