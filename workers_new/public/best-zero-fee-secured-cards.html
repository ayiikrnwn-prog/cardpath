Guide

The $0-annual-fee secured cards, ranked honestly.

Five secured cards cost you nothing per year. We rank them by real value — graduation path, deposit size, and what they actually report.

A $0 annual fee is not automatically a good deal — some free secured cards trap your deposit for years with no graduation path. The real ranking weighs three things: what the card costs (nothing), when you get out (graduation speed), and what it reports (all three bureaus, always).

After those filters, the field narrows to a handful of genuine options.

How it works

Our ranking: (1) Discover it Secured — $0 fee, 2% category cash back, auto graduation reviews at 7 months, deposit back on conversion. (2) Capital One Platinum Secured — $0 fee, deposit from $49, 6-month line reviews. (3) Chime Credit Builder — $0 fee, no credit check, but requires the Chime checking account. (4) Capital One Quicksilver Secured — $0 fee, 1.5% back, $200 deposit. (5) OpenSky — $35 fee, included only because it serves no-bank and bankruptcy cases the others reject.

Who each option fits

Good fit if you:
  • Anyone comparing secured cards by total cost
  • Rebuilders optimizing for graduation, not just approval
Look elsewhere if you:
  • Need no-bank-account or bankruptcy-friendly approval (OpenSky, despite the fee)
  • Have $0 deposit (Self or Credit One — different fee structures)

The honest comparison

The fee trap to avoid: subprime unsecured cards advertising "no deposit" with $75–99 annual fees (Credit One, Indigo) cost $150–198 over two years — more than every card on this list. The deposit path returns its money; the fee path does not.

What to watch out for

The one trap this ranking guards against: cards that advertise "$0 deposit" or "guaranteed" while charging $75–99 annually. Over a two-year rebuild, those fees total $150–198 — more than every card on this list costs combined. The deposit path costs nothing at graduation; the fee path never returns a cent. Another quiet differentiator: reporting completeness. All five cards report to all three bureaus, but Chime's spend-only design reports payments differently — the score effect is equivalent, but utilization modeling differs because there is no fixed line.

Practical next step

Practical next step: run the numbers on your own rebuild — total cost = (annual fee × years) + (deposit you cannot touch). A card that looks expensive per month but returns its deposit at graduation usually wins the two-year math. The quiz applies these same criteria to your deposit budget automatically.

Frequently asked questions

Does checking or applying hurt my credit score?
All five cards offer soft-check pre-qualification or no-check application — checking costs nothing.
What credit score do I need?
None of the five has a hard minimum; OpenSky and Chime perform no credit check at all.
How long does it take to build credit with this card?
All report monthly from the first cycle; 3–6 months of on-time payments is the standard arc.
What happens to my deposit?
All are refundable on good-standing closure or upgrade — Chime is the exception by design (you spend your own deposited funds).
Can I be denied?
Yes on all — but approval rates are highest for the no-credit-check options (Chime, OpenSky).
